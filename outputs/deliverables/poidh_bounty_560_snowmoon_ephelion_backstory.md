### Backstory: The Architect of Unequal Life

Ephelion was not born an aristocrat of the permafrost; he was an archivist in the Outer Sump of the Arctic Empire, tallying caloric deficits during the Long Freeze when the central reactors choked on black ice. His duty was mathematical: measure biological efficiency and flag sub-viable citizens for reclamation. On his seventeenth winter, the formula flagged his younger sister, whose chronic lung fever ranked her in the bottom quartile of biomass yield. When the overseer hesitated, sensing blood loyalty, Ephelion did not plead or bribe. He picked up the stylus, signed her transfer ticket with steady hands, and walked to the furnace gates to retrieve her tarnished silver sewing thimble before the iron doors sealed shut. He learned that day the cardinal law of the Arctic: mercy is a luxury paid for by someone else's starvation.

His ascent through the diplomatic corps was propelled by a ferocious internal contradiction: he loves humanity with a rigor so severe that it resembles cruelty. He despises Veridia’s lush orchards, leisurely blogs, and democratic consensus not because he seeks their conquest, but because he believes comfort is a slow form of suicide. When his face cuts into Veridian dining rooms proclaiming that *"Life is not fair — and should not be fair,"* it is not an imperial threat; it is a desperate warning. His voice is augmented through a surgical throat-valve that clicks faintly whenever his pulse spikes, forcing every cadence to maintain the exact acoustic resonance of inevitable law.

Beneath the tailored velvet of his ambassadorial gauntlet, Ephelion still carries that hollow silver thimble. In the quiet chambers of foreign embassies, when Veridian ministers plead for peace treaties and compromise, his fingers close tightly around the metal, pressing its jagged lip into the nerve of his palm until his knuckles turn porcelain white. He does not want the Arctic Empire to conquer the world; he wants the world to become fierce enough, sharp enough, and unyielding enough to burn the Arctic to ash—so that no child in the dark will ever have to sign another balance sheet of blood.
